# Trail Cards v1 — Normative Specification

Status: DRAFT
Scope: docs/TRAIL_CARDS_V1_SPEC.md
Contract clauses touched: 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9
Depends on: docs/TRAIL_TOPOLOGY_V2_SPEC.md (frozen), CONTRACT.md v1.0 (frozen)

> A trail card is evidence presentation, not evidence authority.

This sentence governs every other clause in this document. Where any
later rule seems to permit a card to stand on its own as proof, this
sentence overrides it.

---

## 1. Purpose

A trail card is a deterministic, one-way visual projection of a
canonical topology/trail export artifact (r4b1t-topology-export or
r4b1t-trail, as applicable). It exists so a trail's shape and
verification status can be looked at and shared without requiring the
recipient to load the full canonical JSON first.

A trail card is not a second evidence format. It does not gain
authority by existing, by being well-formed, by being signed, or by
being widely distributed. Its only claim to trust is a pointer back to
a canonical artifact and, when applicable, a record that verification
of that artifact succeeded at a specific render time.

## 2. Projection direction

Projection is one-way and non-generative:

    canonical artifact (JSON)  --deterministic projection-->  trail card

There is no reverse path. A trail card MUST NOT be treated as an input
to reconstructing, editing, or extending a canonical artifact. A
renderer MUST NOT infer, fill in, or guess canonical fields that are
absent from the source artifact in order to make a card more complete.
Missing source data produces a visibly incomplete or UNVERIFIED card,
never a filled-in one.

## 3. Verification states (frozen, restated)

These three states were frozen prior to this spec and are restated
here as normative for trail cards specifically:

- **VERIFIED** — the source artifact was independently verified at
  render time, using the same verification path as the standalone
  verifier. The card carries the exact digest of the artifact that
  verified.
- **REJECTED** — verification of the source artifact was attempted and
  failed (tampering, digest mismatch, broken commitment chain,
  broken lineage, or any other verifier-detected failure).
- **UNVERIFIED** — verification could not be completed (missing proof
  material, missing parent artifact, unsupported schema version, or
  any condition short of cryptographic rejection). UNVERIFIED is not a
  catch-all for rejection and MUST NOT be used in place of REJECTED
  when the verifier positively detected tampering.

No other verification state exists in v1. A renderer encountering a
condition it cannot classify into one of these three MUST fail closed
into UNVERIFIED, never into VERIFIED.

## 4. What VERIFIED is allowed to mean

A card MAY display a VERIFIED badge or equivalent affirmative
treatment only when all of the following hold simultaneously:

1. The source artifact was run through the independent verifier
   during the same render operation that produced this card (not a
   cached prior result, not a result inherited from a parent trail,
   not an assumption based on file provenance).
2. The card's stored/displayed digest is the exact SHA-256 digest of
   the artifact that verification was run against.
3. The verification covered the full artifact the card claims to
   summarize, not a truncated or partial read of it.

A card MUST NOT claim VERIFIED on the basis of:

- having been "generated by r4b1t" or any trusted tool,
- a prior verification result that is not re-checked at this render,
- the card's own internal consistency (a card can be internally
  well-formed and still summarize a rejected or fabricated artifact),
- possession of a digest string with no accompanying verification
  step having actually run.

## 5. Detached cards carry no authority

A trail card image (screenshot, exported PNG/SVG, pasted-in
rendering, or any form detached from live re-verification) is
presentation only. It MUST NOT be treated, by r4b1t or by any
downstream consumer of this spec, as proof that the underlying trail
verified — only the act of independently verifying the referenced
artifact (by digest) establishes that. A detached card's VERIFIED
badge documents a past claim, not a current fact. Any UI or copy that
displays a detached card SHOULD make this distinction visible (e.g.
"verified as of render" phrasing) rather than implying an ongoing
guarantee.

This clause exists specifically so a screenshot cannot travel with
more authority than it earned.

## 6. Diagnostic rendering for REJECTED / UNVERIFIED

REJECTED and UNVERIFIED cards MUST render in a structurally distinct
format from VERIFIED cards — not merely a different color or badge
on an otherwise identical layout. At minimum, a diagnostic card MUST
include:

- a clear non-color-dependent label of its state (e.g. the words
  "REJECTED" / "UNVERIFIED" as text, not only a red/yellow color cue),
- the failure or incompleteness reason as reported by the verifier,
- the digest the artifact *claimed*, distinguished from any digest
  that was independently confirmed,
- an explicit disclaimer: "THIS CARD DOES NOT ESTABLISH TRAIL
  INTEGRITY."

Diagnostic rendering is observation-only. Rendering a REJECTED or
UNVERIFIED artifact MUST NOT write, cache, or otherwise admit that
artifact into the verified local atlas, regardless of how the render
was triggered (manual render, import, bundle inspection, or share
handoff receipt).

## 7. Proof state is never color-only

Because trail cards are the artifact most likely to be viewed as a
static image (screenshots, shared links, printed/exported form) where
color may be lost, altered, or misperceived, proof state MUST be
conveyed through text and/or structural layout, with color used only
as reinforcement. This applies identically to desktop and mobile
shells. A card that only distinguishes VERIFIED from REJECTED by hue
is non-compliant.

## 8. Portable bundle

A portable bundle is the pairing of {canonical source artifact JSON,
projection/card} for offline handoff. The bundle:

- MUST contain the unmodified canonical artifact alongside the
  projection, not a summary or subset of it,
- MUST allow the recipient to independently re-verify using only the
  bundle's contents and the standalone verifier (no network
  dependency, no account, no r4b1t service call),
- MUST NOT be treated as a second evidence format distinct from the
  canonical artifact — the bundle's evidentiary weight is entirely
  the canonical artifact's; the card component remains presentation.

## 9. Share boundary (normative)

> Sharing may transfer an artifact or bundle to a recipient. It must
> not create a browsable collection, ranking surface, popularity
> signal, recommendation surface, or input to future route selection.

This is normative, not advisory. Concretely, in scope for v1 and any
future revision unless this clause is explicitly amended:

- Forbidden: a "recent shares" list, a public/semi-public gallery of
  cards, view counts, like/star counts, trending or "most shared"
  surfaces, follower/following constructs, or any mechanism that lets
  shared cards be discovered by anyone other than a recipient the
  sharer explicitly sent it to.
- Forbidden: using share events, share counts, or card-view telemetry
  as input — direct or derived — to route selection, wear, sampling,
  or any other selection-adjacent mechanism.
- Permitted: point-to-point handoff of a single artifact or bundle
  (file export, direct link to a specific artifact by digest, copy to
  clipboard, platform share sheet targeting a specific recipient/app).
- Permitted: the recipient independently verifying and re-rendering
  the shared artifact locally.

Any future feature that would violate this clause requires a contract
amendment, not a trail-cards spec revision.

## 10. Cross-shell consistency

Desktop and mobile shells MUST present identical evidence semantics
for a given artifact: the same verification states, the same
disclaimer text, the same digest, and the same share boundary.
Layout, sizing, and interaction affordances may differ per shell;
what a card is allowed to claim may not.

## 11. Non-goals for v1

Explicitly out of scope, consistent with Contract clause 6 (features
extend the proof surface, not the selection surface):

- Card templates driven by engagement, popularity, or behavioral
  signals.
- Multi-artifact aggregation into a single card (comparison is a
  separate future feature family with its own spec).
- Any server-side rendering or storage of cards that would make
  r4b1t itself a distribution surface rather than a handoff tool.

## 12. Relationship to the projection schema

This spec constrains what the projection schema (r4b1t-trail-card/v0.1)
is permitted to encode. Where the schema and this spec appear to
conflict, this spec governs, and the schema must be revised — the
schema is a mechanical encoding of these rules, not an independent
source of them.
